# Phase 2: Competitive Research

## Direct Competitors
Inside Labs operates in a niche — destination engagement platforms specifically for tourism. Most competitors are either broader hospitality SaaS (booking/PMS) or general marketing automation. Inside Labs' closest competitors are:

### Traveltek
- Light corporate SaaS look, blue/white, stock photography
- Focus on booking engine, not engagement

### ITI Digital
- Destination marketing platform, content-focused
- Light theme, very traditional B2B layout
- Not visually distinctive

### Visit Group (now partner, not competitor)
- Clean Scandinavian SaaS design, light and modern
- Good reference for what "professional tourism SaaS" looks like
- But not bold or memorable

## Industry Design Gap
- **The herd pattern:** Tourism tech websites are ALL light theme, blue/teal accents, stock mountain photography, soft/warm/inviting. They look like they're selling vacations, not software.
- **The unclaimed territory:** Dark, bold, tech-forward aesthetic. Treating the software as the hero — showing the platform's intelligence, not the mountains it serves.
- **Inside Labs' opportunity:** Be the first tourism tech company that looks and feels like a serious tech platform. Think Linear or Vercel energy, but for tourism. The mountains come through in the motif and copy, not in stock photography.

## Cross-Industry Design References

### Linear (linearapp.com)
- Dark, restrained, precise
- Scroll-triggered reveals, minimal color, content-first
- **Element to borrow:** Visual restraint — letting the product speak

### Stripe (stripe.com)
- Bento grid product showcase, embedded UI mockups in cards
- Rich card interiors with gradient meshes
- **Element to borrow:** Expanding tab-card product showcase — click to reveal, progressive disclosure

### Vercel (vercel.com)
- Dark theme with white/hot accents
- Dramatic, confident, developer-focused
- **Element to borrow:** The "intelligence visualization" — their deployment network animation

### Netflix
- Dark + red is their entire identity
- Premium, bold, entertainment-as-tech
- **Element to borrow:** Red as a power accent on dark — used sparingly for maximum impact

## Chosen Aesthetic Direction
**Dark + Red: "Alpine Intelligence"**
- Theme: Dark, bold, and confident. A tech platform that happens to serve tourism, not a tourism company that built some tech.
- Color: Near-black background with red accent — premium, distinctive, unmistakable
- Typography: Sharp geometric display, clean body, technical mono
- Layout: Bento grid for Omni Suite, standard grid elsewhere
- Motion: Restrained reveals, card hover lifts, animated mesh visualization
- Signature element: Intelligence mesh — geodesic wireframe network representing the Omni Suite as a living brain connecting destinations

## Motion & Animation
- **Scroll reveal:** opacity 0→1, translateY 28px→0, 0.7s cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), stagger 0.06s, IntersectionObserver threshold 0.12, fire once
- **Card hover:** translateY(-3px), box-shadow deepen to `0 20px 50px rgba(0,0,0,0.35)`, border-color to `rgba(230, 57, 70, 0.3)`, 0.4s same easing
- **Button hover (primary):** brightness 1.12, translateY(-1px), box-shadow `0 12px 40px rgba(230, 57, 70, 0.15)`
- **Button hover (secondary):** border-color → accent, text-color → white
- **Counter animation:** requestAnimationFrame, cubic-out easing, 1.8s duration
- **Nav transition:** transparent→blur backdrop on scroll, padding reduces, border-bottom appears, 0.5s ease

## Signature Motif: Intelligence Mesh
- **What:** A geodesic wireframe network — one central breathing core (the Omni Suite "brain") with satellite nodes connected by lines with flowing particles
- **Where:** Hero section (right 60% of viewport), possibly subtle echo in CTA section background
- **Implementation:** Three.js or CSS/SVG — central icosphere slowly rotating + breathing (scale 1.0↔1.02), satellite wireframes in orbital paths, connection lines with gradient (red near center → white at edges), flowing particle dots along lines
- **The narrative:** The central core IS the Omni Suite intelligence. Satellites represent destinations, guests, data streams. Flowing particles represent data moving through the system. It's not decoration — it's a diagram of what the product does.

**Navigation:**
- Fixed, z-index 999

**Section Headers:**
- Label: JetBrains Mono, 11px, `--accent`, uppercase, letterspacing 0.1em
- Heading: Outfit, clamp(30-50px), weight 700, `--text`, letterspacing -0.03em
- Subhead: DM Sans, 17px, `--text-muted`, max-width 500px

**Cards:**
- Background: `--bg-card`
- Border: 1px `--border`
- Padding: 32px

# Appendix A: Intelligence Mesh — Hero Visualization Spec

## Concept
The hero visualization represents the Omni Suite as a living intelligence network. It is NOT decoration — it is a visual diagram of the product's architecture: a central brain (the platform) connected to satellite nodes (destinations, guests, data streams) with data flowing between them.

This same mesh pattern should be adapted for use on **naly.dev** (different color palette, different meaning, same structural DNA).

## Composition

### Central Core — "The Brain"
- **Shape:** Icosphere / geodesic sphere wireframe (~40-50 polygonal faces)
- **Size:** Dominant — roughly 200-280px diameter at desktop
- **Position:** Right 55-65% of the viewport, vertically centered with the hero headline
- **Wireframe style:** Thin lines (0.5-1px), white/light (`#f0f0f5`) with subtle red inner glow
- **Animation:**
  - Slow Y-axis rotation: ~20-30s per full revolution
  - Breathing: scale oscillates 1.0 → 1.02 → 1.0, 4-6s cycle, ease-in-out
  - Inner glow pulse: `box-shadow` or radial gradient behind it, red at low opacity, pulsing 0.08 → 0.15 → 0.08 opacity, 3-4s cycle
- **Narrative:** This IS the Omni Suite. The intelligence engine.

### Satellite Nodes
- **Count:** 5-7 satellites (not more — clarity over density)
- **Shapes:** Smaller wireframe polyhedra — mix of:
  - Octahedron (8 faces, simple)
  - Icosahedron (20 faces, more complex)
  - Dodecahedron (12 faces, mid-complexity)
- **Sizes:** Varied — 30px to 80px diameter, creating visual hierarchy
- **Position:** Distributed asymmetrically around center. NOT evenly spaced. Some closer, some further. Clustered more toward the right side (don't overlap headline).
- **Wireframe style:** Same thin lines, but lower opacity (0.4-0.7 depending on "distance" from center)
- **Animation:**
  - Slow orbital motion around center — not circular, slightly elliptical, varied speeds
  - Each satellite has its own rotation speed and axis
  - Furthest satellites move slowest, closest move faster (parallax depth)
- **Narrative:** Each satellite represents a destination, a guest segment, a data source.

### Connection Lines
- **From:** Center of core to center of each satellite
- **Style:** Thin lines (0.5-1px), slightly curved (not perfectly straight — use quadratic bezier)
- **Color:** Gradient from `#e63946` (red, near core) → `#f0f0f5` (white, at satellite)
- **Opacity:** 0.2-0.4 (subtle, not overpowering)
- **Animation:** Lines should feel alive, not static. Options:
  - Dashed lines with animated `stroke-dashoffset` (flowing toward center)
  - OR solid lines with subtle opacity pulse

### Flowing Particles
- **What:** Small dots (2-4px) that travel along the connection lines
- **Color:** `#e63946` (red) with a small glow/blur
- **Count:** 1-2 particles per connection line at any given time
- **Speed:** Moderate — ~3-5s to travel full length of a line
- **Direction:** Bidirectional — some flow toward center (data in), some flow outward (engagement out)
- **Easing:** Linear or very slight ease-in-out
- **Narrative:** This is data flowing through the system. Intelligence in motion.

### Depth & Atmosphere
- **Background particles:** Very small (1-2px), very faint (0.05-0.1 opacity) dots scattered in the background area, drifting slowly. Creates sense of a larger network beyond what's visible.
- **Depth of field:** Satellites further from center should be slightly more transparent and smaller, creating a 3D depth effect even in 2D
- **Viewport boundary:** The visualization should extend slightly beyond the visible area on the right edge, suggesting the network continues beyond what we can see

## Technical Implementation Options

### Option A: Three.js (recommended for production)
```
- Scene with PerspectiveCamera, subtle orbit controls (auto-rotate only)
- Central icosphere: THREE.IcosahedronGeometry(radius, detail=2), wireframe material
- Satellites: varied THREE.xxxGeometry, wireframe material, orbital animation via requestAnimationFrame
- Connection lines: THREE.Line with gradient via vertex colors
- Particles: THREE.Points or small spheres animated along paths
- Renderer: WebGLRenderer with alpha: true for transparent bg
```

### Option B: CSS/SVG (lighter, fallback)
```
- SVG wireframe shapes pre-computed, animated with CSS transforms
- Connection lines as SVG <line> or <path> elements
- Particles as small <circle> elements animated along <path> with CSS offset-path
- Pros: No JS dependency, lighter
- Cons: Less fluid rotation, harder to achieve 3D depth
```

### Option C: Canvas 2D (middle ground)
```
- 2D projection of 3D wireframe geometry
- requestAnimationFrame render loop
- Projected vertices + line drawing
- Particles as small arcs drawn along bezier paths
- Pros: Good perf, no library dependency
- Cons: More manual math
```

## Pattern Description

Replace the static bento grid for "Meet the Omni Suite" with Stripe's expanding tab-card pattern. The user sees 3 product names as tabs/headers. Clicking one smoothly expands that product's card to reveal full content, while the others collapse. Only one is open at a time.

## Animation Spec

### Transition Timing
- **Duration:** 0.5s
- **Easing:** `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` (same as site-wide easing)
- **Properties animated:**
  - `flex-grow` (or `width`) of each card — expanded card grows, siblings shrink
  - `opacity` of content inside — collapsed content fades out first (0.15s), expanded content fades in after (0.15s delay)
  - `transform: translateY` of inner content — subtle slide-up as it appears

### Expanded Card State
- **Width:** ~65-70% of container
- **Content:** Fully visible — badge, headline, description, visual mockup, CTA
- **Visual mockup:** Embedded mini UI specific to each product (see content architecture)
- **Red accent indicator:** Small red dot (●) or red left-border bar on the active product name
- **Background:** `--bg-card` with very subtle gradient mesh (accent color at ~0.04 opacity)

### Collapsed Card State
- **Width:** ~15-17.5% of container each
- **Content:** Only the product name, rotated 0° (horizontal) or vertical text, centered
- **Text style:** Outfit, 600 weight, `--text-muted`, uppercase
- **Hover:** Text brightens to `--text`, subtle background shift
- **Cursor:** pointer
- **Border-right:** 1px `--border` separating collapsed tabs

### Mobile Behavior (< 768px)
- Switch to vertical accordion: cards stack vertically
- Expanded card shows full content
- Collapsed cards show just the header bar with a chevron
- Tap to toggle (not hover)

### Key Detail — The Mockup Visuals
Each expanded card contains a mini UI mockup that is NOT an image — it's built in code (divs, styled elements). This:
1. Keeps everything resolution-independent
2. Allows subtle animation (progress bars filling, numbers counting)
3. Matches the design tokens exactly
4. Is more impressive than a screenshot

These mockups should use the same color tokens as the rest of the site but with slightly varied opacity to feel like a "screen within a screen."

## Why This Is Better Than Bento Grid

The bento grid shows all three products simultaneously, which:
- Overwhelms the viewer with information
- Makes all three products feel equally important (they're not — Explore is the hero)
- Doesn't create a moment of discovery

The expanding cards:
- Focus attention on ONE product at a time
- Create interaction and agency (the user chooses what to explore)
- Provide progressive disclosure (see the name → click → see the details)
- Feel premium and intentional (the animation sells quality)
- Default to Engage (entry tier) so the natural reading order goes Engage → Explore → Endeavor
